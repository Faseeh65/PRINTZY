## PART 1: Printzy.site audit (what I found)

**What is already good**
- Clean title and meta description on home, products, about. Open Graph and Twitter tags present. GTM installed. `en_PK` locale set.
- Clear quote flow and WhatsApp button on every page.

**What is hurting SEO and trust**
1. **No indexable product pages.** Footer and cards link to `/quote?product=cups` etc. Google has nothing to rank for "custom paper cups Pakistan". This is the biggest problem.
2. **Thin copy.** Each product is one sentence. No sizes, materials, GSM, print method, MOQ, lead time, price guidance.
3. **About page is ~70 words.** No company story, address, factory, years, team, equipment, process proof.
4. **Vague proof words.** "Low MOQ", "Premium print", "Fast turnaround" with no numbers. The stats row showed "0+ Brands served / 0h" (probably a counter animation, but verify it never shows 0 to crawlers or slow connections).
5. **FAQ answers did not appear** in the page text I could read (only the 7 questions). If they sit in a JS accordion that renders empty, Google may not see them. Verify.
6. **One testimonial** only (Hamza R.). No logos, no ratings, no Google reviews, no case studies.
7. **No location signals.** No address, no city pages, no Google Business Profile link, no map. Gmail address only.
8. **Same images reused** across different products (cups image on napkins/sleeves, food image on takeaway/pizza). Looks template-made and weakens trust.
9. **No blog, no guides, no comparison pages, no price/MOQ explainer.** Nothing to attract buyers who are still researching.
10. **No policies beyond privacy/terms.** No return/reprint policy, no sample policy, no payment terms page.
11. `.site` domain and Gmail address feel less established than a `.pk` or `.com` plus a business email. Consider getting `printzy.pk` / `.com` and `sales@yourdomain`.

## PART 2: Competitor teardown (custompack.pk) and where you can beat it

**What they do well (copy the strategy, not the words)**
- Price and MOQ in the title tag ("from Rs 8/pc, MOQ 500"). That wins the click.
- Every category has: specs (sizes), MOQ, who it is for, use cases, FAQ block, price-band table, related guides, comparison pages ("Paper cup vs PET glass").
- Dedicated pages for: quote calculator, request sample, artwork guide, food-grade inks, export, bulk/corporate orders, and city pages (Lahore, Karachi, Islamabad, Faisalabad, Quetta).
- Real factory address in Lahore, Mon-Sat hours, response-time promise (10-15 min), sample-first workflow, return policy, 7 social profiles.
- FAQs are detailed (MOQ tiers, UV vs screen printing, file formats, timelines by city) and include one Roman Urdu question that matches how locals search.

**Their weaknesses (your openings)**
- **Product gap:** their categories are beverage and disposable items (cups, glasses, bowls, buckets, straws, lids). I saw **no boxes, pizza boxes, mailer/gift boxes, takeaway sets, paper bags beyond kraft food bags, sleeves or stickers.** That is your territory.
- **Price inconsistency:** one page says "from Rs 8/pc", another says Rs 12-30/pc at 500 pcs. Buyers notice. Publish ONE honest price logic.
- **Production time inconsistency:** 3-5 days in FAQ vs 3-7 days in footer.
- **Big compliance claims** ("FDA-safe", "certified") are easy to challenge. Only claim what you can document.
- Heavy keyword stuffing in meta keywords and "popular searches" blocks reads spammy to some buyers.

**Your positioning:** "Complete branded packaging for food brands: boxes + cups + bags + napkins, designed and delivered consistently." Competitor sells cups; you sell the **whole table-to-doorstep set**.

## PART 3: Other custom printing sites (patterns worth borrowing)

I only saw search snippets and directory listings for these, not full audits, so treat this as pattern guidance:
- **PakFactory:** leads with customer reviews about print quality and easy process.
- **Refine Packaging:** ranks with educational guides (printing methods: digital vs offset vs flexo, when to use each).
- **Pakistani box sellers (e.g. Elegant Packages):** list every product type, promise free digital proofs and online quotes.
- **Packlane-style sites:** guest/educational content on design and pricing trade-offs.
- **SEO guidance for packaging firms:** target long-tail, use-case and city keywords instead of only "custom packaging"; build E-E-A-T (experience, expertise, authority, trust) with real proof; publish buying guides.

**Takeaway:** winners combine (a) one strong page per product, (b) education content, (c) visible proof (real photos, reviews, address), (d) location pages.

## PART 6: Technical SEO items (for Antigravity to implement)
- Unique `<title>` and meta description per page; one H1 per page; logical H2/H3.
- Canonical tags; `sitemap.xml`; `robots.txt`; submit to Google Search Console and Bing Webmaster.
- Server-side rendering or static generation so all text is in the HTML (check with "View source" or `curl`).
- JSON-LD: `Organization` + `LocalBusiness` (name, address, phone, hours, sameAs), `Product` (no fake ratings), `FAQPage`, `BreadcrumbList`, `Article` for blog.
- Images: WebP, descriptive file names and alt text, width/height set, lazy-load below the fold. Core Web Vitals: aim for LCP under 2.5s on mobile.
- Internal linking: every product page links to related products, industries and 1-2 guides; breadcrumb navigation.
- Google Analytics/GTM events for quote form submit and WhatsApp click.
- Never publish schema ratings or review counts that are not real.
